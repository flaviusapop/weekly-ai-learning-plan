Edition of Sun, Nov 1, 2026

Weekly AI learning plan for Nov 1–7, about 10 hours

I based the taste check on your X bookmarks from Oct 21–30 (harnesses, software-factory loops, evals, shared context between agents, Grok Bot agent teams). Your Substack saves had not reached me when I wrote this, so Substack status is pending/not read. Everything below is new long-form from roughly Oct 13 – Oct 31. Several picks come from the AI Engineer NYC conference (Oct 13–14), which had a finance track; those links go to the talk page, which has the write-up and a "Watch" link that opens the livestream at the right minute. Every link was checked and loads.

Must Watch (3)

  1. Uber's AI infra, from the inside, Uday Kiran Medisetty (Uber Distinguished Engineer) on The Pragmatic Engineer, about 1h 34m new. The most complete tour this month of how a large company really runs agents: one model gateway, a wrapper that moves skills and settings between Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode, spend tiers, PRs tagged by type, and an honest account of code review becoming the bottleneck.
  2. The AI Sandwich, Agent Primitives, and Designing with AI, Trevin Chow on Agents and Engineers ep 22, about 1h 14m new. A product leader with 25 years in the job who now maintains the Compound Engineering plugin: humans define the work and polish the result, agents do the middle, and cross-model review plus shared context between agents (his Energon project) fill the gaps.
  3. How a Trading Firm Ships New AI Models the Same Day, Noyan Tokgozoglu (Optiver) on the AI Engineer channel, about 23m new. A market maker's AI platform: golden sets from trading teams to test every new model, an inference gateway with budgets, a harness-neutral "meta harness", and how they got spend back under control.

1) AI agents and workflows (about 3h)

  • How I Build with AI Coding Agents: What Changed in Eight Months, Victor Martin Garcia, about 15m read new. A solo builder moved from approving plans to agreeing on a goal and letting the harness check the work, with agents fixing production errors from Sentry on their own; about 600 commits and 170 deploys in 30 days, and his own attention is now the limit.
  • Balancing Acceleration and Safety for AI Agents, John Feminella (Two Sigma) at AI Engineer NYC, about 19m new. A catastrophic payment mistake used to explain five controls that make agents visible, limit their access and save human attention for the decisions that need it.
  • Trusting Agents in Policy-Driven Workflows, Evan Kormos (Coinbase) at AI Engineer NYC, about 17m new. Fixed approval stages with agents iterating inside each stage, and supervision reduced only when measured confidence supports it; close to what a regulated filing workflow needs.
  • Process Supervision for Agents That Do Long-Horizon Work, Mitchell Troyanovsky (Basis) at AI Engineer NYC, about 19m new. Behaviour specs, trajectory forks and cheap simulations to debug agents that run for hours; the example is an accounting agent that must explain a discrepancy instead of booking an entry to hide it.
  • How Anthropic ships the #1 LLM in the World safely: Separating the Brain from the Hands, Katelyn Lesse and Angela Jiang (Anthropic) on Latent Space, about 1h 47m new. Anthropic staff talking about their own platform, so take the product parts lightly; the useful parts are managed agents, memory, evaluating agents and keeping credentials out of the sandbox. The first 4 minutes are a recorded intro about Anthropic's S-1 and can be skipped.

2) Investing and trading with AI (about 2.2h)

Nothing new this week on order flow or futures scalping specifically (no new Blushing Quants episodes since Sep 23), so these four are the best of the rest.

  • Why Trend Following Might Outlast the AI Arms Race, Mark Rzepczynski on Top Traders Unplugged (Systematic Investor 423), about 1h 13m new. Mostly macro (bond yields, safe havens, commodity shortages); the last 10 minutes argue that similar ML models are crowding trades and shortening how long an edge lasts. If time is short, play only from 1:02:12.
  • A Hedge Fund as a Function, Frank Long (Coatue) at AI Engineer NYC, about 14m new. Models a fund as a function from context to selection, size and timing, then splits it into the parts code, models and people each do best; a clean frame for your own trade-review tooling.
  • Agents Prompt People: Using Agents for Long-Horizon Financial Work, Aman Singh (Arca) on Latent Space, about 26m new. A wealth manager where agents do the background work over days but trades and money movement stay with humans behind fully deterministic guardrails; good on memory per client and a meta-agent that turns advisor feedback into harness changes.
  • Vault: Making Unstructured Data Work for Investing, Aaron Linsky and Sam Green (Bridgewater) at AI Engineer NYC, about 16m new. How Bridgewater gives investment agents the research an investor would read and a memory of what the firm learns, in one research loop.

3) Product and building with AI (about 1.7h)

  • AI for the Fortune 2000: How Tenex Builds an AI-Native SDLC, Alex Lieberman and CJ Hess on Latent Space, about 44m new. Plans, decisions and agent logs checked into the repo next to the code (their "MetaHarness"), why most enterprises still have no agent in production outside engineering, and "you can't automate a process that does not exist".
  • Does Code Quality Even Matter?, Max Kanat-Alexander (Capital One) at AI Engineer NYC, about 16m new. Cheap code makes some programs disposable; for systems people keep changing, AI should make engineering rigor cheaper too.
  • Tokens Are the Cheap Part, Steve Jamison (Jellyfish) at AI Engineer NYC, about 17m new. Adds salary and token spend to get the cost of a merged PR, then checks whether apparent gains hide smaller changes or worse quality; useful numbers for a PO arguing about AI ROI.
  • Please… Enough with the Sloppy, One-Shot SaaS Replacements, Mahmoud Abdelwahab (Railway) at AI Engineer NYC, about 13m new. The product details, reusable patterns and verification loop that made agent-built internal tools pleasant to use rather than merely working.
  • The October inflection point for vibe game development, Simon Willison, about 12m read new. Four browser games built with very little input, with prompts and transcripts; a light read on what "make it a LOT more fun" now does and where taste still matters.
