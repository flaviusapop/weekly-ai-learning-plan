Weekly AI learning plan for Nov 22–28, about 9.5 hours

I based the taste check on your X bookmarks from Nov 4–21: Matt Pocock's skills and review loop, evals built from agent traces, eval-driven development, knowledge factories, cutting CI cost with agents, ready-made Orca bots and proactive agents. Your Substack saves are read separately and were not used here. The search window is roughly Nov 1–22, mostly Nov 16–21. AI Engineer conference talks are left to Brief C on Caught Up, so they are not repeated here. Every link was checked and loads.

This week is shorter on purpose: the agents and product sections had fewer strong long pieces, so nothing was added to fill hours. The trading section is the strongest in weeks, because Rob Carver published a full framework for using AI without fooling yourself in backtests.

Must Watch (3)

  1. Don't Let AI Mark Its Own Investment Homework ft. Rob Carver, Rob Carver with Niels Kaastrup-Larsen on Top Traders Unplugged (SI427), about 1h 15m new. A former Man AHL systematic trader on why the model that built a strategy must never validate it: commercial LLMs leak future data into backtests, so he uses pure sequential out-of-sample tests and an add-only backtest where rejected ideas stay in the history. In his own small test, 3 of 4 AI code-review suggestions were good and the 4th would have broken the code. If time is short, start at 52:23 (designing and testing strategies, then self-driving portfolios and where AI helps).
  2. Watch me work: a 21,000-line PR with my skills, Matt Pocock on X, about 1h 10m from your saves. A full working session, not a talk: domain-driven design with /grill-with-docs to settle the language first, then building, /retro to turn what went wrong into better skills, and his own review of the work. The best way to see where a human still sits in his pipeline, instead of the repackaged "1 prompt" threads about it.
  3. Scaling Linear's GTM to $100M ARR, Cristina Cordova (COO, Linear) on Latent Space, about 1h 24m new. More than half of Linear's tickets are now written by agents (about 3% a year ago), why they still do not let an LLM answer support tickets alone, and running sales with no SDRs. Useful for a product owner: how a product team changes its backlog and support rules once agents write the tickets. She runs Linear, so take the product praise lightly.

1) AI agents and workflows (about 1h)

  • How to build evals for an agent, starting with its traces, Hamza Tahir (@htahir111) on X, about 16m read from your saves. A worked example on a LangGraph expense-review agent: read traces, name the failure modes, turn them into evals, and how to tell when the eval itself is wrong. The most practical evals piece of the week.
  • Eval-driven development: how to choose model, retrieval, and design by testing instead of guessing, Sergii Makarevich (@sermakarevich) on X, about 35m read from your saves. A long X article on picking the model, the retrieval setup and the agent design by running evals, not by opinion. Long; read it after the Tahir piece, which is the hands-on version.
  • How to build a knowledge factory, Harrison Chase (@hwchase17) on X, about 7m read from your saves. The learning loop behind LangChain's internal GTM and content agents: how agent runs feed a growing store of reusable knowledge instead of one-off chats. He is LangChain's CEO and links his own products, so read it for the pattern, not the tools.

2) Investing and trading with AI (about 1.1h)

  • AI for systematic traders - this is the least stupid way to do it., Rob Carver on This Blog is Systematic, about 12m read new. The written version of Must Watch #1, with the full framework: data straight from the source, AI writes only small parameterised forecast code, and a human-written tester with burn-in or fake data for fitting, add-only rules locked by a hashed config, backward-looking fitting, and both the positive and the negative version of every rule. Bad uses are listed too: "tell me what to buy" and AI-written code that touches money.
  • Anthropic: Know Before You IPO, Tanay Jaipuria (Wing VC) on Latent Space, about 54m new. What to look at in Anthropic's numbers now that it trades on Nasdaq (debut Nov 19): revenue mix, compute cost, and how it compares with other software IPOs. Context for the week, not a trading idea.

3) Product and building with AI (about 2h)

  • How I fight token fraud as a solo founder, Claire Vo on How I AI, about 40m new. Six abuse patterns she saw on her own AI product, why she killed free trials, the classification loop she built, and an hourly agent that reviews new signups. Concrete if you ever ship anything with a free tier.
  • Making OpenClaw CI faster and 95% cheaper, Vincent Koc, about 20m read from your saves. Runner-hours down 86.5% and compute value down 95.5%, with the technical appendix and a "try this with your agent" prompt at the end. Blacksmith sponsored the runner capacity, so weigh the vendor parts.
  • Why PMs are suddenly valuable again, Tulsee Doshi (Head of Product, Gemini) on Lenny's Podcast, about 58m new. What a product manager does when engineers can build anything fast: taste, evals and deciding what not to ship. She leads a Google product, so skip the Gemini promotion.

4) Design and B2B marketing with AI (about 1.7h)

  • How to Win AI Search and Prove it Drives Pipeline, eight B2B marketers on Exit Five Live, about 46m new. One play each, with numbers: only 30% of about 900 surveyed marketers can prove AI search drives pipeline, AirOps' answers page touched more than $700K in closed-won deals, Compt's homepage rewrite got 2.5× more AI citations, and a three-part attribution model. Several speakers sell tools in this space, so keep the methods and skip the pitches. Nov 12.
  • Nate Parrott on designing with Claude Design, Nate Parrott (Anthropic designer) on Peter Yang's Behind the Craft, about 42m new. Give the model context first (mood boards, past work), ask for 10 options and riff on them, start from wireframes, and rebuild screens from code. He works on the product he demos, so take the tool praise lightly; the habits work in any tool. Nov 15.
  • How I design in Figma with Claude Code, Sil on Into Design Systems, about 10m read new. Her own benchmark of an open-source Figma CLI against the official Figma MCP (1.70M vs 3.53M tokens on 26 tasks), the 200-calls-a-day MCP limit, and why describing your variables and loading brand tokens made the pages on-brand. Ends with a conference promo. Nov 3.
  • Five examples of how designers are integrating AI into their workflows, Jess Eddy on Everyday UX, about 5m read new. Five short real cases: a Figma MCP flow, a component library mapped in a CLAUDE.md, an overnight brand exploration, deck building, and a moodboard-to-Framer skill. A quick menu of things to copy. Nov 10.
