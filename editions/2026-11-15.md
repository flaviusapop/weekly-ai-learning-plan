## Must Watch (3)

1. [Dave Mabe - AI Overconfidence in Systematic Trading](https://podcast.ditchinghourly.com/episodes/dave-mabe-ai-overconfidence-in-systematic-trading), Dave Mabe on Ditching Hourly, about 58m, new. A systematic day trader of 25 years (20–25 models a day, former CTO of Trade Ideas) on how Claude Code entered his workflow: unit tests first, moving from manual approval to auto mode for his trade client, and why AI overconfidence in backtesting is the expensive risk. He sells a backtesting product, so take those parts lightly; the useful part is how he turns expert judgment into guardrails and questions instead of trusting one answer.
2. [How Stanford Teaches AI-Native Engineering](https://www.latent.space/p/one_million_ai_engineer_special_how_stanford_teaches_ai-native_engineering_mihail_eric_monaco/), Mihail Eric (Stanford CS146S, head of AI at Monaco) on Latent Space, about 2h 1m, new. What to assign when an agent finishes last year's homework in one prompt, then how his own team runs it: agent workspaces per agent, "buy to learn, build to keep", making repos agent-ready, an on-call agent with Datadog and GitHub access, and what to do when code review becomes the bottleneck. If time is short, start at 59:21 (inside Monaco's engineering team).
3. [How this founder built a better Duolingo with Codex](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-this-founder-built-a-better-duolingo), William Imoh (Hackmamba) on How I AI, about 39m, new. A former product manager who never wrote Swift ships a native voice app with Codex, then shows the Lord of the Rings–themed Grok Bot "fellowship" that helps him run a team of about 25 as a solo founder: one role per bot, access to almost everything except one thing, and what he still does himself. The closest match this week to how you run Grok Bot teams.

## 1) AI agents and workflows (about 1.8h)

- [Grok vs. Dot vs. Muse vs. Claude Duet: which one crushes the competition?](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/grok-vs-dot-vs-muse-vs-claude-duet), Claire Vo on How I AI, about 35m, new. Grok Bot, Dot, Muse and Claude Duet on nine real tasks (Slack reply, Sentry bug, Figma thumbnail, inbox triage, shopping, a launch video), operated through computer use and scored live. Useful as an outside view of where Grok Bot wins or loses against the others; Oct 28, not used in earlier plans.
- [The Home Repo Pattern](https://www.aihero.dev/the-home-repo-pattern), Matt Pocock, about 8m read, from your X saves. One repo holds your skills, AGENTS.md and shared docs, with one pointer per other repo; sessions start there. Includes a setup prompt that only reads until you approve the plan, and how it scales from 20 to hundreds of repos.
- [Graph Engineering: Draw the Work Before You Build the Agents](https://x.com/distortgeekin/status/2120494371287990372), distort (@distortgeekin) on X, about 9m read, from your X saves. Draw steps, handoffs, state and stop conditions first, then count the agents; the five shapes (chain, router, fan-out, manager and workers, loop) and why Anthropic's "split" and Cognition's "don't build multi-agents" were both right for different work. A short X article, not a study.
- [Delegation, Agent Router, and AI Traffic](https://agentsandengineers.com/episodes/0024-delegation-agent-router-and-ai-traffic/), Erica Hughberg (Tetrate, Agent Router maintainer) on Agents and Engineers ep 24, about 1h 30m, new. Managing coding agents as delegation ("you can't control everything, but you are responsible for everything"), why agents miss undocumented decisions and whole-system context, planning more, and why most "agents" are just scheduled tasks in a general harness. The first 54 minutes are the part for you; the rest is proxy and networking detail.

## 2) Investing and trading with AI (about 3h)

Still no new Blushing Quants episode since Sep 23, but this week has a direct ES/NQ piece.

- [Building Trading Tools with Claude (and What It Got Wrong)](https://tradingedgejournal.com/blog/building-trading-tools-with-claude/), Hank Fortuna on TradingEdge Journal, about 12m read, new. An ES and NQ trader who could barely code now trades from an order-flow footprint chart Claude built, with 16 years of 1-minute bars and a nightly research loop. Concrete numbers: an EMA19 pullback read +3.26 ticks per trade on 1-minute bars and −0.94 on real tick prints; one tick of worse fill halved the edge; 105 filter groups, zero survivors. The fixes are a random-entry control, tick re-scoring when the stop is near a bar's range, and a locked-away holdout. The site sells a trading journal; skip the trial box.
- [Why Would Anthropic Go Public Now?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P735f1xlpL0), Jamin Ball (Altimeter, Clouded Judgement) on Latent Space, about 1h 42m, new. Reading Anthropic's S-1 like an investor: the roadshow and Thanksgiving window, IPOs as a way to finance data centers, revenue growth against the cost of serving Claude, how fast a frontier model depreciates, and the $80.7B net loss and convertible notes. He discloses Altimeter's position in Anthropic.
- [Why Trend Following Isn't Crash Insurance](https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/podcast/why-trend-following-isnt-crash-insurance-ft-philippe-jordan/), Philippe Jordan (CFM) on Top Traders Unplugged, about 1h 7m, new. Fund flows move prices, crowding and forced deleveraging behind the 2007 quant quake, and price impact by turnover. Around 25:00 he says agents already test papers at CFM and will start trading, and explains why an AI model with two years of data can still be worth under 1% of risk as diversification.

## 3) Product and building with AI (about 2h)

- [Designing for Agents](https://www.latent.space/p/resend/), Zeno Rocha (Resend) on Latent Space, about 1h 26m, new. Why Claude Code picked Resend first in 64 of 102 email recommendations, and what "agent experience" means in practice: a `.md` version of the pricing page, an MCP server that grew from about 20 to 80+ tools, and opening collapsed tool calls to see how much of a result the model needs. A founder talking about his product, so take the product parts lightly; the useful part is designing a product that agents choose and can use.
- [Claude Code + Obsidian: My family's AI brain](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/claude-code-obsidian-my-familys-ai), Scott Farrar on How I AI, about 34m, new. A Markdown folder viewed in Obsidian becomes a household brain (appliances, warranties, insurance, a shared to-do file, a wall display with "recent wins"). A small, honest example of moving from chat that forgets to files the agent and a partner both maintain.

## 4) Design and B2B marketing with AI (about 2.2h)

A new track this week for design and B2B marketing workflows.

- [Clint Sutherland: Turning Figma Into an AI-Powered Workstation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OCFnZbPsQ_4), Clint Sutherland (TON Studio) on Beyond The Brief (OVRFLO), about 1h 10m, new. A brand and digital designer walks through the Figma system he built with Claude: brand-language files the model updates as you work, a machine-readable strategy-deck template he can populate in a couple of hours instead of a week, and auto-filled proposals and statements of work. He is clear that the system does not do the strategy for you, and that you have to read what the model writes — he has already sent clients hallucinated email. He plans to sell the deck system, so take those parts lightly; the useful part is how he puts brand rules into Figma so the model fills the template instead of designing from a blank prompt. Oct 13, used as the one longer evergreen item.
- [AI-assisted creativity: 6 plays from real B2B teams (Exit Five Live)](https://exitfive.com/podcast/ai-assisted-creativity-6-plays-from-real-b2b-teams-exit-five-live/), six B2B marketers on Exit Five Live, about 53m, new. Short, concrete workflows: a Claude skill that builds a sales-call brief from approved positioning, a brand-skills library so anyone can make an on-brand landing page, and a positioning file that every AI tool starts from. The show has several sponsors; one speaker, Mike Fredrick, is SVP of Creative at Walker Sands, which also sponsored the episode, so go easy on the Figma Weave production demo. Oct 15.
- [How to Build Marketing Agents Inside Your Team's Claude](https://drewcbrucker.com/field-notes/marketing-agents-in-claude/), Drew Brucker, about 7m read, new. A consultant describes 25 builds in ten weeks for Limble, a B2B maintenance-software company, after a rebrand: one brand context file every agent reads first, then agents that audit stale logos and copy, score pages, and index 732 public pages. He sells this service, so take the close lightly; the useful part is the order (context, then training, then agents) and the rule that the agents live in the team's own Claude workspace. Oct 27.
