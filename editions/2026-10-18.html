Edition of Sun, Oct 18, 2026

Weekly AI learning plan for Oct 18–24, about 10 hours

I based the taste check on your recent X bookmarks (personal agents, Grok Bot and Notion memory setups, harness engineering, Claude Code and Codex workflows, Lee Knowlton's notes on AI-verifiable work). Your Substack saves are all from July or earlier, so they only informed taste. Everything below is fresh long-form from roughly the last three weeks. I skipped the Luke Pierce masterclass you saved, because it is a module from his paid course for AI agency owners.

Must Watch (3)

  1. Why Every Traded Personal Agents for One Company Agent, Dan Shipper with Willie Williams on The Every Podcast #110, about 34m new. An honest look at why everyone's personal agents decayed and what one shared agent does better. Every launched its own agent that week, so expect a little promotion.
  2. Why every company now needs to think and operate like a lab team, Josh Woodward (Google Labs) on Lenny's Podcast, about 1h 2m new. How to run experiments, spot product-market fit early, and know when to kill an idea.
  3. The Science and Engineering of Agent Work, Hilary Mason, Eric Ma, Bryan Bischof and Tomasz Tunguz on Vanishing Gradients, about 2h 22m new. Four heavy users show their real agent skills, memory setups and evals.

1) AI agents and workflows (about 1.5h more)

  • The 4-Step Agentic Engineering Workflow I Use to Ship Real Apps, Ras Mic on Behind the Craft (Peter Yang), about 47m new. Parallel agents, validation loops, agent PR review, and the one step he never delegates.
  • nobody should type code by hand anymore. here's what's left., Geoffrey Huntley, about 31m read new. An opinionated take on vision, verification and putting the whole system under test.
  • What is Codemode, Armin Ronacher, about 10m read new. Why agents should write scripts instead of loading lots of tools into context.

2) Investing and trading with AI (about 2.9h)

This was the thinnest topic again. I found nothing long-form and credible on AI for discretionary order flow, and I left out the "24/7 AI trading bot" hype on X.

  • Backtest in Sierra Chart with Python (Why I Don't Use Replay Mode), Drew Thomas at One Person Trade Desk, about 23m with the written companion new. Why Sierra's fast replay modes look ahead when a strategy reads a second chart, and a Claude Code check for lookahead bias. He sells a community, but the method shown is free.
  • How He Automated His Way to 3,500 Trades a Year, Dave Mabe on Trading Nut #343, about 40m new. Going from discretionary to 25 automated strategies, and how to make Claude check its own backtest code.
  • Build a Financial Analyst Harness That You Can Trust, Francesco Galletta and Felippe Felisola Caso (Monzo) on the AI Engineer channel, about 1h 51m new. A bank team builds an analyst harness step by step with verification built in.

3) Product and building with AI (about 1.9h more)

  • Generating Opportunity Solution Trees with AI, Teresa Torres with the Vistaly team on Just Now Possible, about 1h 20m new. How a discovery tool rebuilt itself around AI interview synthesis, evals and repair loops.
  • I Spent Two Years Building the Median, Hariprasad Sudharshan on the AI Engineer channel, about 36m new. Why prompt-to-product gives you the median app, and three questions to ask before you build.

Suggested order

  • Sun: Must Watch #1 (Every), Codemode and the Median talk.
  • Mon: Must Watch #2 (Josh Woodward) and the Sierra Chart backtest video and article.
  • Tue: First half of Must Watch #3 (Vanishing Gradients).
  • Wed: Second half of Must Watch #3 and the Huntley essay.
  • Thu: Ras Mic and Dave Mabe.
  • Fri: Vistaly on Just Now Possible.
  • Sat: The Monzo analyst harness workshop.

The total is about 10.3 hours. Tell me if you want next week heavier on markets or lighter on agent deep dives.
